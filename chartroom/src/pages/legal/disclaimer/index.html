Study use only

This site is for learning to read charts and airspace as you prepare for the private pilot knowledge test and checkride. Do not use it for navigation, flight planning or any decision you make in the air.

Charts and airspace can be out of date

FAA sectional charts and airspace data are republished every 56 days. Chartroom shows the data as the FAA’s public services serve it when you load the page, and it can lag the current cycle. Before any flight, use current official charts, the Chart Supplement, NOTAMs and TFRs, and a weather briefing from an approved source.

The display is not exact

  • The Height slider stretches airspace vertically so layers are easier to see. Heights are not to scale.
  • Floors given above ground level are drawn from sea level, because the airspace map has no terrain.
  • Areas with no charted ceiling are drawn up to 18,000 feet MSL, where Class A begins.
  • Practice answers use the airspace data at a single point. They can differ from what a chart shows after a recent change.
  • The base map is simplified and labels can overlap.

Your responsibility

Under 14 CFR 91.103, each pilot in command must become familiar with all available information about a flight before it begins. Chartroom is not a substitute for that duty.
